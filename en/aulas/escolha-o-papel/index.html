Lesson 1 · 16 min

The filter: four roles and a trap

The 43 opportunities look like 43 different businesses. Almost all of them fit into a few boxes. Building became easy and cheap, so anyone who only delivers the building competes on price. What a company buys is trust: someone who diagnoses, reviews, follows up and teaches. This lesson builds the filter you will use throughout the whole course.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Recognize the five boxes of the filter
  2. 2Cut opportunities using the five criteria
  3. 3Find your unfair advantage in your own background
  4. 4Write an offer without technical jargon

Goal: classify an opportunity into one of the 4 roles or into the building that loses value, and cut the candidates using the 5 criteria.

01 · The five-box filter

Every opportunity falls into one of them. Click each box.

More about each role in the Professional 2030 area.

Four roles sell trust. The fifth box is not forbidden: it is a piece of a service, not the whole service.

02 · Before you choose

Three honest questions that come before the list.

1. Four paths

Founder (full control, zero income for months), number 2 partner (half the stage, shared income), intrapreneur (an AI project from the inside, with a salary) and weekend (a day job, a business at night).

All four teach almost the same thing. Only the founder path charges the full price right away. If people depend on your income, starting with the lowest-risk path is sequencing, not cowardice.

2. Fuel

Write down what you are running from and what you are running toward. Without both sides named, giving up tends to arrive in the first weeks without results.

Too much fuel also has a cost: set a time when work ends, even if it is one day a week.

3. The ladder

Every opportunity has four steps. The first three trade hours for money and are mandatory: nobody teaches a method they have never carried out.

Notice step 4: teaching the method. That is exactly where the Trainer and the Mentor come in.

4. AI in a business

Saying "I run an AI agency" doesn't open doors: clients hear that every week. What works is finding the most expensive pain and solving it, with AI if it's the best tool. Sometimes it isn't.

Test: write your offer in one sentence and delete the technical words. If what's left is a sentence the owner of a bakery understands, you have an offer.

Compare"I deploy AI in your company" gets ignored. "You get back 12 hours a week" gets read.

03 · The five criteria

Every candidate goes through them. Two negative answers and it's off the list.

1. Does the client have money?

Someone barely keeping their head above water rarely invests in improvement. Prefer those who are already making revenue.

2. Is the pain expensive and measurable?

If you can't estimate how much the problem costs per month, you won't be able to defend your work either.

3. Do you have an unfair advantage?

Industry background, contacts, or a skill most people don't have. Without that, you compete on price alone.

4. Can you prove it in one week?

If the first demo takes three months, you give up before the payoff. And the opportunity you can stand to repeat is the one that builds a track record.

Mistake that breaks the filter: picking the most exciting one instead of the most likely one.

04 · Templates to copy

Replace the text between < > with your own. Use it in an AI assistant of your choice.

Prompt · unfair advantage inventory
You are a positioning consultant. I'm going to give you my complete professional biography, including
odd jobs, bad jobs, failed businesses and hobbies. Don't praise anything.

My biography: <list each job, odd job, business or hobby on one line, with how long it lasted>

Do three things:
1. For each item, write in one sentence what it taught me about PEOPLE, about PROCESS or about an INDUSTRY.
   If it taught nothing useful, write "disposable" and move on.
2. List the 3 industries where I can join a conversation and sound like an insider, not an outsider.
   Justify each one with the item from the biography that supports it.
3. For industry number 1, list the 5 expensive pains I have probably already seen happen in there
   and that could be solved today with automation or AI.
Be specific. No "communication skills".

05 · Practice

Do this before lesson 2. The table stays with you through lesson 8.

1 · Unfair advantage inventory

25 min

Run the prompt with your complete biography, including the jobs you're embarrassed to mention: they tend to be the most useful.

Done when: you have 3 industries, each tied to a concrete item from your biography, and none of them fits just anyone.

2 · Filter table open

15 min

Copy the table into a spreadsheet or note. Fill in the first row with the opportunity that attracts you most today, classifying the box and the five criteria.

Done when: the table exists, has the box and 5-criteria columns, and the first row is filled in with Y/N in all of them.

06 · Quick test

Question 2 of 4

Two opportunities attract you equally. What decides first?

Question 3 of 4

Question 4 of 4

Building becomes a componentIt isn't forbidden: it's part of a trust-based service, not the whole service.
Five criteriaMoney, measured pain, unfair advantage, proof in one week and tolerance for repetition.
